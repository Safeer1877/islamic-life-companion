# Islamic Life-Management System: Functional Foundation Research

*Desk-based synthesis prepared 24 October 2026. No participants were recruited, no scholars were consulted and nothing was tested. Every scenario is hypothetical. This is not a fatwa. It marks exactly where qualified scholarly review is required before anything religious is built on it.*

## 0. How to read this document

**Evidence labels**

- **\[Q\]** Qur'anic text. **\[H\]** hadith, cited by collection and number. **\[F\]** a position of a named school, council or scholar. **\[Hist\]** a historical example (descriptive, not normative). **\[B\]** behavioural-science finding. **\[Hyp\]** untested hypothesis. **\[Rec\]** my recommendation.
- **✔** = source page retrieved and read during this research session. **○** = cited from the standard collection or journal by number or DOI but not re-opened this session; a qualified reviewer must confirm the number, wording and grading before anything depends on it. Hadith are paraphrased, not quoted.

**Known gap in coverage.** The religious sources I could work with are predominantly Sunni (the four Sunni schools, Sunni hadith collections). Ja'fari, Zaydi and Ibadi positions are not represented here. Decision 1 (universal) and Decision 3 (legitimate differences) cannot be honoured until scholars from those traditions review the content. This is listed again in Deliverable 6.

## 1. Findings in brief

1. **The core object is a commitment with a strength label, not a task.** Islamic sources distinguish obligation, concession, recommendation, permission and disagreement. That gradation is richer than a to-do list and maps directly onto planning constraints (fixed, flexible, optional, undecided).
2. **The most reusable principle is plural rights.** The Salman and Abu al-Darda' narration (Bukhari 1968 ✔) names Allah, oneself and family as each having a due, and the Prophet ﷺ endorsed the correction. It gives no ratios. The system must therefore not invent a "correct balance" formula; balance is judged by the person, with the rights made visible.
3. **Obligations are fixed in core but flexible in mode; voluntary worship scales to capacity.** The sources repeatedly protect obligations (Bukhari 6502 ○) while scaling the voluntary (Q 73:20 ○; Bukhari 39, 43, 1150 ○). That supports one central rule: defend the obligations, flex the voluntary, never the reverse.
4. **The largest religious-misrepresentation risks are defaults, not exotic rulings.** Prayer-time calculation method, the madhhab-dependent Asr time, combining prayers for hardship, Jumu'ah exemptions, travel distance, women's mosque attendance and Ramadan night-prayer format are all places where a silent default would present one legitimate position as the answer. Rule: **no silent defaults on disputed matters.**
5. **Behavioural evidence is supportive but narrower than it looks.** If-then planning has a medium-to-large effect on goal attainment (d = 0.65, 94 tests; Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 ✔) but mostly in secular and health goals. Habit formation is slow and variable (median 66 days, range 18 to 254; Lally et al. 2010 ✔). JITAI principles (adapt to changing state and receptivity) are well specified, and a 2025 review found adaptivity and receptivity were among the weakest-implemented components ✔. I found **no trial** testing whether prompting improves religious practice or wellbeing in Muslim populations. Absence from one search is not absence from the literature, but nothing here should be described as proven for this use.
6. **Challenge to the concept.** Decision 3 (do not mislead religiously) and Decision 4 (individual control) together imply the system treats the person's chosen positions as inputs, never as outputs. The moment the system "decides" what is permissible it is acting as a mufti. Proposed stance: **a planner operating under a rulebook the person owns**, with scholars (not the system) as the source of religious content.
7. **Challenge to the onboarding decision.** A conversation cannot be both comprehensive and light. Section 3 proposes about ten core questions before a first draft plan, with everything else asked at the moment it would change a decision.
8. **Challenge to "verified mosque schedules."** No one currently verifies iqama times at scale and they change. Treat them as user-confirmed data with a visible "last confirmed" date, never as system knowledge.
9. **Challenge to "silent by default."** It is a hypothesis. Decision 5 says engagement is not success, but silence could reduce obligation-fulfilment for people who want reminders. The resolution is user-chosen assistance level, offered openly at the start.
10. **Several requested items cannot be validated by reading.** Whether people understand the layer separation, whether the first draft plan fits real days, and whether any prompt helps are empirical questions (Deliverable 6).

## 2. Deliverable 1: Islamic Life Framework

### 2.1 Classification used by the system (design categories, not fiqh rulings)

The schools use finer categories (the Hanafi wajib/fard distinction, for example). These are planning categories only.

- **A. Binding, time-bound or condition-bound** (five daily prayers within their windows; Ramadan fasting; Jumu'ah; zakat; hajj when able; duties to dependents). Immovable by the system.
- **B. Binding in core, flexible in mode** (concessions for illness, travel, fear, sleep or forgetfulness, menstruation). Flexible only through the person's own circumstances and chosen scholarly position.
- **C. Recommended** (sunnah prayers, voluntary fasting, Qur'an and dhikr, congregation where not held obligatory, night prayer). Scalable and skippable without failure.
- **D. Permissible arrangements** (when to work, study, exercise, rest; how to organise a household). Where most planning happens and the person decides.
- **E. Disputed** (see 2.5). Never resolved by the system.
- **F. Illustration only** (historical examples, modern behavioural interpretations). Never presented as instruction.

### 2.2 Organising principle: plural rights

- **Source.** Bukhari 1968 ✔ and 6139 ✔ (https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6139); parallel in Tirmidhi 2413 ✔ (listed as a parallel). Salman found Abu al-Darda' fasting and praying through the night while his wife was neglected; he made him eat, sleep, and wake for prayer only in the last part of the night, saying that his Lord, his self and his family each have a right, and to give each its due. The Prophet ﷺ said Salman spoke the truth. A classical gloss on the body's right (feeding, resting, treating illness) is in the SeekersGuidance answer ✔ (https://seekersguidance.org/answers/hadith/what-does-your-self-has-a-right-over-you-mean/).
- **Establishes.** Rights are plural and real; excess in voluntary worship that neglects body or family is corrected by the Prophet's own endorsement; the ordering of the night (sleep first, rise late) is an example of sequencing, not a timetable.
- **Does not establish.** Any ratio of time among rights; that these three are exhaustive (some narrations add the guest); how to rank when they truly collide; that the Abu al-Darda' case generalises to every voluntary practice.
- **Related texts.** Three men asking about the Prophet's worship and the reply that he prays and sleeps, fasts and breaks fast, marries (Bukhari 5063 ○, Muslim 1401 ○). Religion is ease and no one overburdens himself without being overcome (Bukhari 39 ○). Hanzala told to give an hour to this and an hour to that (Muslim 2750 ○). Health and free time as commonly squandered blessings (Bukhari 6412 ○).
- **Juristic generalisation** \[F ○\]: rights of Allah are built on tolerance and rights of people on strictness. A commonly cited maxim, not a text, with exceptions. It should not be used to rank a worship activity above a person's right.
- **Use \[Rec\].** The system's "balance view" lists the person's active rights (worship, self, family, work, others) and shows which are currently unprovided for. It never outputs a balance score.

### 2.3 Domain analysis

**Domain 1: Worship and religious obligations**

- *Principles.* Prayer is prescribed at set times (Q 4:103 ○); windows are described in Muslim 612 ○ and 613 ○. Jumu'ah (Q 62:9-10 ○) is followed by an explicit permission to disperse and seek Allah's bounty. Ramadan fasting with exemptions for illness and travel (Q 2:183-185 ○).
- *Concessions.* Sick people pray standing, then sitting, then on the side (Bukhari 1117 ○). Travel shortening (Q 4:101 ○; Muslim 686 ○). Sleeping through or forgetting a prayer: pray on remembering (Bukhari 597 ○, Muslim 684 ○); no blame in sleep (Muslim 681 ○). Menstruating women do not pray and do not make up prayers (Muslim 335 ○, broadly agreed \[F\]).
- *Combining prayers.* Ibn 'Abbas reports the Prophet ﷺ combined Zuhr/'Asr and Maghrib/'Isha in Madinah without fear or rain, "so his community would not be put in hardship" (Muslim 705 ✔; Nasa'i 602 ✔ https://sunnah.com/nasai:602; Tirmidhi 187 ✔). Interpretation is divided \[F ○\]: the Hanafi school limits true combining to 'Arafah and Muzdalifah and reads the report as combining in form only (delaying the first prayer to its end and praying the second at its start); other schools allow combining for defined reasons (travel, illness, heavy rain) with varying breadth; a minority view in classical commentary (reported, for example, by al-Nawawi on this hadith) allows it for genuine need if not habitual. A variant in Bukhari 543 ✔ records an early speculation that it was a rainy night, which shows the disagreement is old. **The system must not suggest combining as a scheduling tool for work or shifts.** It can state that scholars differ and refer.
- *Congregation.* Merit: Bukhari 645 ○, Muslim 650 ○. The blind man refused a concession because he could hear the call (Muslim 653 ○). Ruling is divided between individual obligation, communal obligation and emphasised sunnah \[F ○\]. Women may attend mosques (Bukhari 900 ○, Muslim 442 ○), attendance is not obligatory, and Jumu'ah is not obligatory on women, travellers or the sick per the majority \[F ○\].
- *Prayer times.* Boundaries depend on method. Fajr/Isha twilight angles vary by body (15° to 19.5° appear across publishers; MWL uses 18° for Fajr ✔ https://fiqh.islamonline.net/en/praying-and-fasting-at-high-latitudes/ ; a table of regional conventions is at https://salahtimes.com/faq ✔). Jordan's Dar al-Ifta advises following the officially recognised timetable where one lives and does not give weight to the 15° view ✔ (https://aliftaa.jo/en-fatwas/3970/rss.aspx); the ECFR calls high-latitude timing a matter of ijtihad and endorses the MWL Fiqh Academy method with room for other estimations ✔. Asr start differs between the Hanafi school (shadow twice the object) and the majority (once) \[F ○\].
- *Establishes.* The obligations are real and time-bound; concessions are textual; windows are method-dependent. *Does not establish.* Exact minute boundaries; universal mosque attendance; a single method for high latitudes.
- *Use \[Rec\].* Prayer is a set of anchors in time, not a tracker. The system shows windows under the person's chosen method; it never tallies prayers.

**Domain 2: Livelihood, education and knowledge**

- *Principles.* Seek provision (Q 62:10 ○, 67:15 ○); eating from one's own labour is praised (Bukhari 2072 ○); neglecting those one is responsible for is a sin (Muslim 996 ○; Abu Dawud 1692 ○). Learning what is needed to fulfil obligations is individually required; wider knowledge carries communal and recommended weight (Q 20:114 ○; Muslim 2699 ○). "Seeking knowledge is an obligation on every Muslim" (Ibn Majah 224 ○) is widely cited but its chains are criticised; its grading is disputed \[F ○\].
- *Hist.* Umar and an Ansari neighbour alternated days between the Prophet's gatherings and their work, reporting to each other (Bukhari 89 ○). 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf asked only to be shown the market on arrival in Madinah (Bukhari 2048 ○). **Shows** that work and learning were divided, not stacked. **Does not show** that this division suits any given modern job.
- *Does not establish.* Working hours, whether an employer must accommodate prayer (jurisdiction-dependent), or that secular study is worship (a juristic question \[F\]).
- *Use \[Rec\].* Work and study are hard practical constraints (Class 2). The system plans around them and never judges them.

**Domain 3: Family and caregiving**

- *Principles.* The best of you are best to their families (Tirmidhi 3895 ○). The Prophet ﷺ helped in the household (Bukhari 676 ○). Everyone is a guardian responsible for their charge (Bukhari 893 ○, Muslim 1829 ○). Parents (Q 17:23-24 ○; Bukhari 527 ○), neighbours (Bukhari 6014 ○), the widow and the poor (Bukhari 5353 ○). No soul is burdened beyond capacity, including in child-rearing arrangements (Q 2:233 ○).
- *Establishes.* Family care is a duty and a right, not an interruption of worship.
- *Does not establish.* A division of household labour for modern families, how to rank parent against spouse against child in conflict, or any schedule for time with family. Those are individual and family decisions (and, where unclear, scholar-advised).
- *Interpretively sensitive \[F ○\].* The narration restricting a woman's voluntary fasting while her husband is present without his permission (Bukhari 5192 ○) is understood differently by scholars. The system must not present it as a universal planning rule.

**Domain 4: Body, sleep and health**

- *Principles.* The body has a right (Bukhari 1968 ✔; also Bukhari 1153 ○, 1975 ○, Muslim 1159 ○ for 'Abd Allah ibn 'Amr, whose eyes also had a right). Sleep and rest are part of the created order (Q 78:9-11 ○). Seek treatment (Muslim 2204 ○, Tirmidhi 2038 ○). Moderation in food and drink (Q 7:31 ○, Tirmidhi 2380 ○). A person drowsy in prayer should sleep (Bukhari 212 ○).
- *Establishes.* Care for the body is part of religious duty; sleep is legitimate; excess is reproved.
- *Does not establish.* A sleep schedule, the Prophet's night prayer as normative for everyone, or when sleep deprivation from necessary work becomes blameworthy.
- *Use \[Rec\].* Sleep is a capacity constraint (Class 5) that modulates Class 3 items and can change the mode of Class A items (seated prayer, shorter practice) but not their existence.

**Domain 5: Community**

- *Principles.* Whoever fulfils his brother's need, Allah fulfils his need (Bukhari 2442 ○; Muslim 2699 ○, 2580 ○). Many community duties are communal (fard kifaya) rather than individual \[F ○\].
- *Does not establish.* A quota of volunteering. The system must not create one.

**Domain 6: Recreation, rest and digital wellbeing**

- *Principles.* Hanzala is told an hour for this and an hour for that (Muslim 2750 ○). The Abyssinians' play in the mosque (Bukhari 454 ○, 950 ○). Racing 'A'isha (Abu Dawud 2578 ○, graded sahih by some). Part of the excellence of a person's Islam is leaving what does not concern him (Tirmidhi 2317 ○, hasan; contested by some) and the believers avoid idle talk (Q 23:3 ○).
- *Establishes.* Lawful recreation is endorsed; time is a trust.
- *Does not establish.* Any rule about specific apps, games or screen-time limits.
- *Use \[Rec\].* "Unwanted distraction" is defined by the person, not by the system.

**Domain 7: Character and conduct**

- *Principles.* Nothing is heavier on the scale than good character (Tirmidhi 2002 ○). Do not be angry (Bukhari 6116 ○). Ridicule and suspicion are warned against (Q 49:11-12 ○). Actions are by intentions (Bukhari 1 ○); the Qur'an warns against showing off in prayer (Q 107:4-6 ○).
- *Use \[Rec\].* Do not track or score character. Anything shareable or ranked about worship invites riya' and turns intention into performance. This is the religious reason behind no streaks, leaderboards or visible tallies.

**Domain 8: Moderation and time (cross-cutting)**

- *Principles.* A middle community (Q 2:143 ○). Do not forbid the good things Allah made lawful (Q 5:87 ○); monasticism was not prescribed (Q 57:27 ○). The most beloved deeds are the most regular even if small (Bukhari 6464 ○, 43 ○). The night-prayer rope between pillars: pray while energetic, rest when tired (Bukhari 1150 ○). Allah eases night recitation for those who are ill, travelling for livelihood or fighting (Q 73:20 ○).
- *Use \[Rec\].* Every voluntary commitment carries two sizes: usual and hard-week minimum, and shrinking is never a failure.

### 2.4 Historical examples and what they do not show

- **Salman and Abu al-Darda'** (Bukhari 1968 ✔): a correction of excess, not a schedule.
- **Umar and the Ansari neighbour** (Bukhari 89 ○): turn-taking, not a universal template.
- **'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf** (Bukhari 2048 ○): trade was a normal part of a devout life.
- **al-Ghazali, Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din**, the book on arranging litanies and the book on earning and livelihood (numbering varies by edition; confirm, ○): a rich classical model of structured days, written for committed seekers within a Sufi pedagogy, with some narrations that hadith critics (al-'Iraqi's takhrij) graded weak. Use as a source of ideas and vocabulary, not as normative scheduling.
- **Women scholars of hadith**: Mohammad Akram Nadwi's *Al-Muhaddithat* (Interface, 2007) \[Hist ○\] documents thousands of women transmitters and teachers. Women's religious life in the sources was not mosque-attendance only.
- **What none of these show**: that any single pattern applies to a modern shift worker, a student, or a mother of small children.

### 2.5 Unresolved scholarly questions affecting daily planning

The system must neither resolve nor hide these:

1. Fajr/Isha angles; Asr shadow rule; end of Isha; high-latitude methods.
2. Combining prayers for work, need or hardship.
3. Congregation: individual or communal obligation.
4. Jumu'ah for workers, students, women and travellers; whether work or study excuses attendance.
5. Travel distance and duration thresholds; when a resident becomes a traveller.
6. Fasting for pregnant and nursing women (fidyah, qada or both) and for those with chronic conditions.
7. Making up deliberately missed prayers.
8. Night prayer and taraweeh format and count.
9. Employee duty to an employer versus prayer windows (negotiation, short breaks, jurisdictional law).
10. Voluntary fasting and spousal permission (Bukhari 5192 ○ and its interpretation).
11. Positions of Ja'fari, Zaydi and Ibadi traditions on timing, combining and fasting (not covered here).

## 3. Deliverable 2: Personal Understanding Framework

### 3.1 Design stance and its evidence

The onboarding is a conversation with a budget, not a form. It has three stages:

- **Stage 1, before the first draft plan:** about eleven prompts, roughly four minutes. Only what the first plan cannot be built without.
- **Stage 2, just in time:** one question at a time, asked only when the answer would change a specific decision (a trip is entered, Ramadan approaches, a concession becomes relevant).
- **Stage 3, on demand:** deeper settings the person opens themselves.

**Evidence and its limits**

- Stated intentions predict behaviour only partly: changing intentions by a medium-to-large amount changes behaviour by a smaller amount (Webb & Sheeran 2006, *Psychological Bulletin* 132(2):249-268 ○). Stated routines are intentions, so the first plan is a **hypothesis**, labelled Draft.
- People underestimate how long things take (planning fallacy; Buehler, Griffin & Ross 1994, *JPSP* 67(3):366-381 ○). Time estimates the person gives should not be treated as measurements.
- Self-reported digital media use often diverges from logged use (Parry et al. 2021, *Nature Human Behaviour* ○). The system should not build precise phone-use plans from self-report, and must not pull logs automatically (see 3.6).
- Autonomy support matters for sustained motivation (Ryan & Deci 2000, *American Psychologist* 55(1):68-78 ○, https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.55.1.68).
- Religion and health data are special categories under GDPR Article 9 ○ (https://gdpr-info.eu/art-9-gdpr/). I am not giving legal advice; a legal review is required. The design consequence is the same either way: ask less, make every sensitive item skippable, keep only what a decision needs.
- **Not found:** peer-reviewed evidence on the best number of onboarding questions, or on drop-off in life-planning onboarding. The "about eleven" budget is **\[Hyp\]** to be tested (Deliverable 6).

### 3.2 Rules that apply to every question

- **U1.** One line of purpose precedes each question ("I'm asking so I can ...").
- **U2.** Every question offers *Skip*, *Ask me later* and, where sensitive, *Prefer not to say*. Skipping never disables core function.
- **U3.** "Not sure" is a valid answer with a defined fallback (shown in each entry).
- **U4.** Choices before free text. Free text only where choices cannot capture the answer.
- **U5.** No question asks for another person's name or identity.
- **U6.** No answer receives a religious judgement, praise or warning.
- **U7.** The plan after Stage 1 is a Draft. Each item shows whether it came from "you told me", "I'm assuming" or "I don't know yet".

**Opening (a statement, not a question):** "I can help organise your days around your responsibilities, including worship if you want that. You decide how much I do. You can skip anything, change anything later, and nothing here is scored. What I make first is a draft for you to correct."

### 3.3 Stage 1 questions

Each entry: **Ask** / **Needs** / **Decision it changes** / **Answers and effects** / **Sensitivity, skip, defer, staleness**.

**Q1. How much help do you want?**

- *Options:* Quiet planner (I look when I choose) · Gentle reminders · Active partner · Just let me look around.
- *Needs:* the person's intervention preference. *Decision:* whether the system may speak unprompted at all, and the daily proactive-message ceiling.
- *Effects:* Quiet planner = no unprompted messages; Gentle = reminders only for items the person chose; Active = reorganisation proposals and check-ins within a stated ceiling \[Hyp: default ceiling of three per day, to be tested\]. Not sure → Quiet.
- *Sensitivity:* none. *Skip:* yes, defaults to Quiet. *Defer:* no, it governs everything after. *Staleness:* changeable at any time; re-offered once if the person dismisses several suggestions (see 4.5).

**Q2. Is there something that feels out of balance right now?** (optional)

- *Options (choose up to three):* sleep · prayer rhythm · family time · study or work focus · health and movement · phone use · learning · community · not sure · nothing, just organise.
- *Needs:* where the person feels tension, **not** a ranking of virtues. *Decision:* which domains the draft plan treats as priorities and which it leaves alone.
- *Why this wording:* asking what people "should" prioritise invites socially desirable answers. Asking where tension is felt is closer to a real constraint. **\[Hyp\]**
- *Sensitivity:* low. *Skip:* yes → no priority weighting, plan shows constraints only. *Staleness:* volatile; offered again after major life events.

**Q3. Which is closest to your week?**

- *Options:* Fixed hours · Shifts or rotating roster · Variable or self-employed · Student · Mostly caregiving · A mix · Between jobs or retired.
- *Needs:* the **type of regularity**, not the details. *Decision:* whether the system plans from clock times (fixed), from per-roster blocks (rotating), from protected anchors and windows (variable), from a term timetable (student) or from interruptible windows (caregiving).
- *Conditional follow-ups (one at most is asked):*
  - Fixed: rough start, finish and commute in half-hour bands → hard practical block; checks overlap with prayer windows and Friday.
  - Rotating: "How do you learn your schedule: repeating pattern, weekly roster, or short notice?" and "Is there a block when you are necessarily asleep?" → sleep anchors move with each block; the person's "day" starts at wake, not midnight; optional morning items are hidden after night shifts.
  - Variable: "Is any part of the day or week reliably yours?" → protected anchors; plan expressed as "after Maghrib" style cues rather than clock times \[Hyp: cues survive schedule drift better than times\].
  - Student: "Does your timetable change by day or term? Any exam periods?" → term flag; offers a lighter mode later.
  - Caregiving: "When are you least able to be interrupted, and when most interrupted?" → windows, not times; interruptible items only.
  - Frequent travel: **deferred** to the moment a trip is entered.
- *Sensitivity:* low. *Skip:* yes → treated as variable; only prayer anchors and sleep are planned. *Staleness:* volatile; re-asked on new job, new term, roster change or two corrections in two weeks.

**Q4. What cannot move, and what could?**

- *Needs:* the split between hard practical blocks and flexible ones. *Decision:* which items the system may propose moving. *Effects:* hard blocks are never reorganised; flexible blocks may be proposed for movement only with approval; unmarked blocks are treated as hard (the safer error).
- *Sensitivity:* low. *Skip:* yes → everything hard. *Staleness:* corrected whenever the person drags or edits a block.

**Q5. When do you usually sleep, and how predictable is it?**

- *Options:* steady · depends on shifts · unpredictable (young children, health, other) · prefer not to say.
- *Follow-up only if "unpredictable":* "Would you like me to plan around that rather than try to change it?"
- *Needs:* a sleep window or the fact that none exists. *Decision:* what optional activity can sit before the first prayer or late at night.
- *Effects:* steady → optional items can sit on either side of a protected window; shifts → window is per-roster; unpredictable → **no** optional pre-dawn or late-night items, and the plan offers a minimum version of everything. Grounding: Bukhari 1968 ✔ (sleep first, rise in the last part of the night) and Bukhari 212 ○ (a drowsy person should sleep).
- *Sensitivity:* low to moderate (can reveal health). *Skip:* yes → no sleep-dependent suggestions. *Staleness:* volatile. The system never diagnoses; if the person reports poor sleep repeatedly, it offers to lighten the plan, nothing more.

**Q6. Does anyone depend on your time regularly?**

- *Options:* children · an ill or older relative · household or spouse responsibilities · no one · prefer not to say.
- *Follow-up only if yes:* "Which parts of the day are fixed for them (school run, meals, medication) and which are unpredictable?" Children's ages only in bands (under 1, 1-5, school age, teen), and only if it changes the plan.
- *Needs:* fixed care blocks and interruption tolerance. *Decision:* whether items are scheduled as times or as windows; whether reminders are suppressed in unpredictable periods. Grounding: Bukhari 1968 ✔; Muslim 996 ○.
- *Sensitivity:* moderate (family, possibly health). *Skip:* yes. *Staleness:* changes with children's ages, school terms, illness.

**Q7. Which of these would you like me to plan around?** (multi-select)

- *Options:* the five daily prayers · Jumu'ah · fasting (Ramadan or voluntary days) · Qur'an · dhikr and supplication · voluntary prayers · study circle or learning · charity or service · not now · prefer not to say.
- *Needs:* which religious layers to switch on. *Decision:* the whole religious layer. The system **does not infer or audit** observance.
- *Effects:* only selected layers appear. *Not now* or *prefer not to say* → general life planning only, with one line saying practices can be added at any time. No follow-up pressure.
- *Sensitivity:* **high** (special-category data). *Skip:* yes. *Defer:* yes. *Staleness:* changeable instantly; the layer can be paused without explanation.

**Q8. How do you decide when prayer times begin and end?** (asked only if prayers selected)

- *Options:* my local mosque's timetable · a calculation method I follow (list: Muslim World League, ISNA, Umm al-Qura, Egyptian General Authority, Karachi, Moonsighting Committee, Diyanet, other) · what my country's authority publishes · not sure.
- *Follow-ups:* "Which rule for Asr: earlier (majority) or later (Hanafi), or follow my mosque?" Asked only if a method is chosen; and, only if the person's location is where twilight persists in summer, "Which high-latitude rule do you follow, or follow my mosque?"
- *Needs:* the method and Asr rule. *Decision:* every window boundary shown and every "time remaining" statement.
- *Fallback if unknown **\[Rec, needs scholarly review\]:*** show a **cautious window**: start at the latest start among the commonly used methods, end at the earliest end, labelled "This is the span all common methods agree on; confirm with your mosque or scholar." This avoids silently choosing a method while never telling anyone they are in time when some method would say they are not.
- *Sensitivity:* low to moderate. *Skip:* yes (cautious windows). *Staleness:* stable; re-asked only on relocation to a different country or a long stay. Seasonal shifts need no re-asking (windows are recomputed).

**Q9. Do you want to plan around praying with others?** (asked only if prayers or Jumu'ah selected)

- *Options:* at a mosque for some prayers (which?) · Jumu'ah at a mosque · with others at work or home · I prefer alone or it doesn't apply now · not sure.
- *Follow-up only if mosque:* "Which mosque(s), and do you know their times?" with *enter times*, *I'll confirm when I go* or *skip*. If the person wants step-free access or other facilities, see 3.5 (functional needs only).
- *Needs:* a user-supplied place and times. *Decision:* whether to show "congregation opportunity" items and whether a "leave by" suggestion is possible at all.
- *Effects:* times come only from the person; each carries a "last confirmed" date; with no confirmed time the system makes **no** leave-now suggestion. The question is neutral on gender and does not assume mosque attendance for anyone (Bukhari 900 ○: not forbidden; not an obligation on every person \[F ○\]).
- *Sensitivity:* moderate to high (place of worship). Store only what a feature needs. *Skip:* yes. *Staleness:* iqama times drift; update only when the person corrects or after a failed attempt, not on a timer.

**Q10. Beyond what's required, is there something you'd like to make room for?**

- *Options:* Qur'an · dhikr · voluntary prayer · voluntary fasting · study · charity or service · something else · nothing right now.
- *Follow-up for each chosen item:* "What does a usual week look like, and what's the smallest version that still feels like keeping it?" This records **two sizes**: usual and hard-week minimum. Measure in time, not pages or counts, unless the person chooses otherwise.
- *Needs:* intention and sustainable size. *Decision:* what the plan reserves, what it shrinks automatically in a hard week, and how cues are proposed ("after Maghrib" rather than a clock time, subject to the person's approval).
- *Evidence:* if-then planning helps translate intention into action (d = 0.65; Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 ✔), strongest when the person already holds the goal; it does not decide whether the goal is wise. Regularity over volume (Bukhari 6464 ○, 43 ○) supports the two-size design.
- *If the stated load looks unsustainable* (for example heavy voluntary worship plus night shifts plus fasting), the system says so once, citing the rights principle (Bukhari 1968 ✔), and asks whether to start smaller. It does not refuse and it does not nag; for serious health risk it refers to a clinician or scholar.
- *Sensitivity:* high. *Skip:* yes. *Staleness:* volatile; offered again before Ramadan and after life events.

**Q11. About your phone, and when should I stay quiet?**

- *Concern options:* late-night use · during prayer or family time · work or study focus · compulsive checking · nothing to change.
- *Help mode:* do nothing · I'll ask when I want help · remind me at times I choose.
- *Quiet times (multi-select):* work or study blocks · sleep · prayer · family time · custom.
- *Needs:* quiet windows and what "unwanted" means to this person. *Decision:* silence windows, reminder budget, and whether any wind-down prompt exists.
- *Effects:* nothing is measured. No usage logs are read. If the person wants a wind-down prompt they set the bedtime and the system uses it as given.
- *Sensitivity:* low. *Skip:* yes (silence). *Staleness:* revisited when suggestions are repeatedly dismissed.

**Q12. Confirmation (not a question about the person's life).** "Here is what I understood. Tell me what's wrong." Three lists: **You told me**, **I'm assuming**, **I don't know yet**. Edits are one tap. Unknowns stay visible until the person chooses to fill them. Only then does the Draft plan appear.

### 3.4 Stage 2 and 3 questions (asked when needed, not upfront)

- **Which scholar, school or council do you follow for questions like this?** Asked the first time a decision truly depends on a disputed matter (travel, combining, Jumu'ah exemption). Options: my local imam, a named school, a council, not sure. Skippable; the system then presents positions without choosing. *Why deferred:* many people do not know their school's position on a technical point, and asking at onboarding yields noise.
- **A pause, not a tracker.** "Would you like a way to pause prayer and fasting reminders on days you're not required to pray or fast? You don't need to explain." A switch only. No cycle, pregnancy or illness logging.
- **Ramadan (asked about three weeks before):** how sleep will change, what fasting looks like for this person, whether to switch on a Ramadan mode. Fasting suitability (illness, pregnancy, nursing, medication) is referred to a clinician and scholar, never decided.
- **Travel (asked when a trip is entered):** dates, rough distance, whether concession information is wanted at all, and which position they follow. Never assume; never offer combining by default.
- **Exercise, rest and recreation:** "Is there anything you'd like left open or protected for movement, rest or enjoyment?" Open time can be marked "leave empty" and is never filled.
- **Anything the system must never suggest?** A free-text boundary list the person can edit.

### 3.5 Accessibility and health: functional needs only

The system asks about **what is needed**, never **why**. "Would you like me to plan around physical limits or fatigue? Examples: praying seated, limited standing, needing step-free access, resting between activities, medication times. You don't need to give a reason." Responses become capacity constraints (Class 5). Qur'anic and prophetic grounding for ease and capacity: Q 2:286 ○, 22:78 ○, 48:17 ○; Bukhari 1117 ○. The system does not verify accessibility of any building; it can only use what the person has confirmed.

### 3.6 What the system must never infer or collect

**Never infer, even from strong signals**

- How devout the person is, whether they pray or fast, or why they missed anything.
- Sect, school or scholar followed.
- Menstrual cycle, pregnancy, fertility, health or mental-health conditions, disability, sleep disorders.
- Marital or family conflict, finances, immigration status, sexual orientation.
- Spiritual "state" of any kind. Confident wrong inferences in this area are the main way a system like this would become religiously misleading and personally intrusive.

**Never collect passively**

- Phone content, app usage, browsing, messages, contacts, microphone or camera, continuous location, health-sensor data, anything about other people.

**Collect only with a stated reason and opt-in**

- Coarse location (city) for prayer times; precise location only momentarily in travel mode.
- A mosque's identity, only for congregation features.
- Functional accessibility needs.

*Why:* religion and health are sensitive categories; inferred data produces confident errors; and perceived surveillance undermines the autonomy that sustains motivation (Ryan & Deci 2000 ○).

### 3.7 Confirming understanding before consequential recommendations

Every stored fact has a **confidence label**: *Told* (the person said it, still fresh), *Observed* (derived from the person's own corrections and accept/dismiss choices, shown to them), *Assumed* (a default, shown as such), *Unknown*.

- **T1, private and reversible** (reordering optional items, proposing a time for a voluntary activity): apply, show the change, one-tap undo.
- **T2, plan-structural** (changing the sleep window, adding a commitment, using an *Observed* pattern): propose, show what changes, require confirmation.
- **T3, religiously consequential** (any concession, a missed prayer, fasting while ill, combining, Jumu'ah versus work, any disputed item): present sourced information with named positions, ask which position the person follows, do not choose, offer scholar referral.

Consequential recommendations require a *Told* fact. *Observed* patterns can be offered ("I noticed X; should I use it?") but are never applied silently.

### 3.8 When an answer becomes outdated

- Answer types: **Stable** (method, school, city), **Seasonal** (sleep, term timetable), **Volatile** (roster, caregiving pattern). Each fact shows "last confirmed".
- Re-ask **only when:** the person contradicts a stored fact; a life event is declared (new job, term, baby, move, trip, Ramadan); two or more corrections in one area within two weeks; or a plan is about to rely on a Volatile fact that has not been confirmed for a while \[Hyp: about four weeks\].
- **Never** re-ask on a timer for Stable facts, and never re-ask the same question more than once if the person skipped it.
- One question at a time, always with the reason it is being asked.

### 3.9 How answers change the system's decisions: worked demonstrations

- **Rotating night shifts (Q3).** The person's day begins at waking. Optional morning items disappear after night shifts. Zuhr or Asr may fall inside protected sleep, so the system shows the windows and asks, "Would you like a wake-up inside the window, or leave sleep protected?" It states that scholars discuss sleeping through a prayer and that the person decides. Sources: Muslim 681 ○, Bukhari 597 ○.
- **Infant at home (Q6).** Items become windows, not clock times. Minimum sizes are the default view. No clock-time reminders during unpredictable periods.
- **Method unknown (Q8).** Cautious windows appear, with a one-time prompt to specify a method.
- **Asr rule differs.** The Asr window opens at a different time under the earlier and later rules (often by tens of minutes). Choosing the wrong rule shifts every Asr-related statement, which is why Q8 exists and why the cautious window is the fallback.
- **"I prefer alone" or "not applicable" (Q9).** No congregation items appear, and no one is told they could attend.
- **Q7 skipped.** General life planning only. One line says Islamic practice can be added later. No further prompts.
- **"Late-night use" (Q11).** The system asks for a desired bedtime, then only offers a wind-down prompt if the person selected reminders. It measures nothing.
- **"Unpredictable sleep" (Q5).** No optional item before the first prayer; everything shows its minimum size.

## 4. Deliverable 3: Life Decision Framework

### 4.1 Three layers of authority

The system keeps three kinds of statement apart. Every message carries a visible layer tag.

- **Layer R, religious content.** Prayer windows under a stated method, concession information, the map of disputed matters. Sourced, versioned, and reviewed by qualified scholars before use. The system may **present** this layer. It never **issues** a ruling, never derives R from personal data, and never silently chooses between valid positions.
- **Layer P, the person's rulebook.** What the person has declared: method, school or scholar, chosen practices, two-size commitments, boundaries, "never suggest" list. Owned and editable by the person.
- **Layer L, life planning.** Schedule, capacity and behavioural adaptation. It optimises freely **inside** R and P.

**Rules \[Rec\]**

- L cannot alter R. P can narrow L but cannot alter R's content. R is never inferred from L data (no "you seem to skip Fajr, so ...").
- A statement like "Asr window: 15:42 to 17:48, per the method you chose" is R, tagged with the method. "I moved your reading to after Maghrib" is L. "Scholars differ on combining prayers; here are the positions" is R, tagged as disputed.
- *Why:* Decision 3 and Decision 4 both fail if one layer can quietly rewrite another. The separation also makes the system testable for misattribution (does anyone read a planning suggestion as a fatwa?).

### 4.2 Constraint classes

- **Class 1, fixed religious windows** (D1, categories A and B) under the person's method. The system cannot move or skip them. Only the person, invoking a concession in their own rulebook, changes the *mode*.
- **Class 2, hard practical** (shift, lecture, school run, appointment, a care task that cannot wait).
- **Class 3, soft commitments** (voluntary worship, exercise, study goals, social plans).
- **Class 4, preferences** (when the person likes to do things).
- **Class 5, capacity** (sleep, illness, fatigue, caregiving load). Modulates Classes 3 and 4 and can change the *mode* of Class 1 (seated prayer, shorter practice), never its existence.

**Binding:** Classes 1 and 2. **Flexible:** Classes 3 and 4. **Requires human judgement:** conflicts between Class 1 and Class 2, conflicts between rights of different people, and anything in confirmation tier T3.

### 4.3 Resolving conflicts between two important activities

**Step 0, is it a real conflict?** Many conflicts dissolve on inspection: prayer windows are often longer than people assume, and a meeting that ends inside the window is not a conflict. The system shows the slack ("the window runs until 17:48; your meeting ends 16:30") before anything else.

**Step 1, voluntary against anything higher.** The voluntary item yields without discussion. Grounding: the obligatory is the most beloved means of nearness (Bukhari 6502 ○), and the rights narration (Bukhari 1968 ✔). The system offers the minimum size or a different slot, never guilt.

**Step 2, flexible against hard.** The flexible item moves.

**Step 3, hard against hard** (Class 1 vs Class 2, or one person's right against another's). **The system does not rank.** It states the facts (window, slack, duration of overlap), the options the person controls (a short break inside the window, speaking to a supervisor, a different slot, a different location), whether the matter is disputed, and asks what the person wants to do. For religious matters it offers scholar referral (tier T3). It never proposes missing a window as a scheduling option.

**Step 4, the person decides.** The plan follows. If they invoke a concession already in their rulebook, the plan follows that, tagged "per your rulebook."

**Step 5, nothing evaluative is recorded.**

*Illustration (hypothetical).* A 13:00 all-hands meeting overlaps Jumu'ah. Step 0: the meeting length and mosque iqama (if confirmed) are compared. Step 3: the system does not decide whether work or Jumu'ah wins (positions on excuses differ, 2.5 item 4, and employer duty is a jurisdictional and contractual matter). It lays out the facts, offers "prepare a short note to ask your scholar" and "draft a message to your manager" if the person wants either, and waits.

### 4.4 The eight actions and the rules that select them

The actions: **offer information, suggest an alternative, ask for clarification, help reorganise, preserve the routine, abstain, refer, remain silent.** Rule IDs are referenced elsewhere. Thresholds marked \[Hyp\] are untested starting points.

- **R1, silence is the default.** Unless the person's assistance level (Q1) plus a chosen item makes a message due, do nothing.
- **R2, pull before push.** Religious information appears when asked, or when a chosen reminder makes it directly relevant. It is never delivered as exhortation.
- **R3, alternative.** Suggest an alternative only when (a) the existing plan will breach a hard constraint and (b) an option exists wholly inside P and L. Offer at most two.
- **R4, clarification.** Ask only if (a) the answer changes a near-term decision and (b) no *Told* fact exists. One question, with *Skip*.
- **R5, reorganise.** After a change to a hard constraint (roster, trip, illness, new commitment) or on request. Show the diff; T2 approval.
- **R6, preserve.** If the plan still meets every hard constraint and capacity limit, leave it alone. A gap in the day is not a defect. The person may mark time "leave empty" and it is never filled.
- **R7, abstain.** If a decision needs a fact below *Told* confidence and the consequence is T2 or T3, show what is missing instead of guessing.
- **R8, refer.** For T3 topics, show named positions with sources and refer to a scholar. For health or safety (fasting while ill, pregnancy, medication, exhaustion), refer to a clinician as well. If asked "what should I do?", the system says it cannot issue rulings and offers to prepare a short summary the person can take to a scholar.
- **R9, sleep protection.** Optional pre-dawn or late-night items are proposed only if the sleep window is *Told* and adequate by the person's own definition. After night shifts, never.
- **R10, congregation.** A "leave by" suggestion needs a user-confirmed time and the person's own travel estimate. Otherwise abstain.
- **R11, method unknown.** Use the cautious window (Q8). Ask once.
- **R12, concession information.** Offer only when the person's declared context triggers it (travel, illness), their rulebook permits concession information, and the content names the positions. Never offered as a way to make the schedule easier.
- **R13, missed obligation.** No counters, no debt, no judgement. If the person raises a missed prayer, give sourced information (pray on remembering, Bukhari 597 ○, Muslim 684 ○), note that positions differ on deliberate delay, and offer referral.
- **R14, interruption is not failure.** Interrupted items are rescheduled only if the person asks; otherwise they lapse.
- **R15, disruption mode.** See 4.9.
- **R16, voluntary scaling.** During low capacity or disruption, only minimum sizes are shown. Usual sizes return when the person declares the period over.
- **R17, no score.** No metric of devotion: no streaks, totals, rankings, comparisons or badges. Intention and showing off are religious concerns (Bukhari 1 ○; Q 107:4-6 ○). Psychologically, tangible rewards can undermine intrinsic motivation (Deci, Koestner & Ryan 1999, *Psychological Bulletin* 125(6):627-668 ○), with moderators, so this is a caution rather than a proof.
- **R18, harm check.** If a plan the person sets would plausibly harm their body or family (chronic short sleep, heavy fasting during illness), the system voices concern once, cites the rights principle (Bukhari 1968 ✔), and offers a lighter version. It does not forbid and it does not repeat itself. Serious risk → R8.

### 4.5 Silence, dismissals and the reminder budget

**Conditions for silence**

- Quiet times the person set (Q11); disruption mode; a hard block in progress; the system has nothing new; confidence is insufficient; the person dismissed this kind of message recently; a reminder for the same window has already been sent.

**Reminder budget \[Hyp\]**

- A ceiling per day set by the person (default for "Gentle": three). Unused budget does not roll over, and nothing is "bonus" content. Messages about obligations the person opted to be reminded about count against the same budget.

**Dismissals \[Hyp thresholds\]**

- One dismissal is not a signal. Two dismissals of the same *type* within fourteen days stop that type and the system asks once, "Stop these?" After *Stop*, the type returns only if the person asks.

**Long absence \[Hyp\]**

- After about three weeks without interaction, one check-in only: "Stay quiet, keep going, or stop?" Then follow the answer and do not repeat.

**Silence is not abandonment.** Everything remains available on pull.

**Evidence and honest status.** JITAI frameworks treat receptivity and vulnerability as states to be modelled, and the option of **no intervention** is part of the logic (Nahum-Shani et al. 2018 ✔ https://doi.org/10.1007/s12160-016-9830-8; Nahum-Shani, Hekler & Spruijt-Metz 2015 ✔ https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26651462/). A 2025 systematic review of JITAIs in mental health found adaptivity and receptivity under-implemented ✔ (https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2025.1460167/full). Micro-randomised trials exist to test whether a prompt at a decision point helps (Klasnja et al. 2015, *Health Psychology* 34S:1220-1228 ○). Whether silence-first improves wellbeing or obligation-fulfilment here is **\[Hyp\]**.

### 4.6 Adaptation: what triggers reassessment and what does not

**Triggers**

- A correction by the person. A declared life event (job, term, baby, move, illness, trip). A hard-constraint change (new roster, new timetable). A calendar boundary (Ramadan, school term, clock changes). Repeated dismissals of one type. A request.

**Not triggers**

- One missed or dismissed item. One bad night. Weekend variation. First-week variance. How often the app is opened or how long since last use. Seasonal movement of prayer windows (recomputed automatically, not re-planned).

**Learning signals that are allowed**

- Edits and corrections the person makes. Accept, dismiss and snooze of suggestions. Times the person actually moves items to. An explicit "that was too much."

**Not allowed**

- Passive sensing of any kind (3.6).

**Rule for observed patterns \[Hyp\]:** a pattern needs at least three occurrences across at least two weeks **and** the person's confirmation (T2) before it changes the plan. The system words it as a question: "You've moved Qur'an to after Maghrib three times. Make that the default?"

**What habit research implies.** Missing a single opportunity did not significantly slow habit formation in the main study, but very inconsistent performance did (Lally et al. 2010 ✔ https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674 ; discussion by the study's author https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2009/aug/how-long-does-it-take-form-habit). The median time to peak automaticity was 66 days with a range of 18 to 254, and a critical re-reading reports that only a subset of participants fit the model well (secondary source, verify against the paper). Design consequences: no "habit formed" claims in week one, no failure state for a single miss, and no promise that any practice will become automatic.

### 4.7 Preference change versus obligation change

- **Preference change** ("I'd rather read after Fajr"): update P and L immediately (T1).
- **Circumstance change** (illness, newborn, trip, new job): update L; if the person's rulebook allows, offer relevant R-layer information (concessions, positions) as information; never rewrite R.
- **Obligation change:** only the person's recognised scholarly authority can change what is obligatory for them. The system never changes R because of personal data. If the person says "I'm too busy for Fajr right now," the system treats that as a plan statement: it pauses its own reminders without arguing and without declaring a ruling. If the person says "my scholar says I may combine on shift," the system records it in P as *Told, per your scholar*, plans accordingly, and does not verify or endorse it.
- **Pausing the whole religious layer:** done on request, respectfully, with no retention prompts and one line saying it can be resumed.

### 4.8 Behavioural-science mapping, with limits

- **COM-B** (Michie, van Stralen & West 2011 ✔ https://doi.org/10.1186/1748-5908-6-42): diagnose barrier type before intervening (capability, opportunity, motivation). **\[Hyp\]** For the persona groups in this project, most scheduling problems are **opportunity** problems (rosters, caregiving, commute) and some are **capability** problems (not knowing a concession exists, how to pray seated). Motivational prompts aimed at an opportunity problem are the wrong tool. Decision rule: first ask what blocks the activity (not enough time, not sure what is allowed, or does not want to). Time → reorganise; not sure → information or referral; does not want → the person's own call, no nudging.
- **Implementation intentions** (Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 ✔): cue-anchored if-then plans for the person's own chosen items. A 2024 meta-analysis of 642 tests is reported secondhand and should be checked directly ○. Pair each plan with a coping plan ("if my day is disrupted, I do the minimum"; Sniehotta et al. 2005, *Eur J Soc Psychol* 35:565-576 ○). Effects are for goals people already hold; they do not choose goals.
- **JITAI structure** (Nahum-Shani et al. 2018 ✔): decision points (a window opens, a roster changes), tailoring variables (declared quiet times, current block, declared capacity), decision rules (R1 to R18), intervention options (including none), receptivity. Evidence in the field is dominated by physical activity, smoking and mental-health apps. Transfer to worship and family life is unproven.
- **Self-determination theory** (Ryan & Deci 2000 ○): supports autonomy-supportive wording: choice, a reason, no "must" and no "you missed."
- **Digital wellbeing:** the system's own notifications are an attention cost. The reminder budget and silence rules apply to the system first.

### 4.9 Disruption protocol

**Trigger:** the person says something came up, edits a hard block, or declares illness, bereavement or an emergency. **\[Hyp, no direct evidence\]**

1. Freeze proactive messages for the rest of the day (or until the person returns).
2. Show only Class 1 windows (if that layer is on) and Class 2 blocks, in neutral wording.
3. Ask once: "Rebuild today, rebuild tomorrow, or leave things as they are?"
4. Voluntary items collapse to minimum size. No catch-up.
5. After the disruption: do not reschedule missed items unless asked. Offer the usual sizes back only when the person says so.
6. No evaluative record of what was missed.

## 5. Deliverable 4: Real-Life Simulations

**All six people below are invented for design reasoning. They are not research participants, and nothing here is an observation of real behaviour.** Times are illustrative. References to Q-numbers are the onboarding prompts in section 3; R-numbers are the decision rules in section 4.4. The simulations are chosen to stress the framework, so each contains at least one place where the system is wrong or does not know.

### Simulation 1: Maryam, 20, university student (hypothetical)

Third-year engineering student, shared flat, 40-minute bus commute, timetable different every day, thesis deadlines. Prays at the university prayer room. Follows the later (Hanafi) Asr rule. Wants a steadier Fajr and short daily Qur'an. Uses her phone late at night.

**First use.** Q1 *Gentle reminders*. Q2 sleep, phone. Q3 *Student*: timetable varies by day; exams in January → term flag. Q4 lectures and labs hard, study blocks flexible. Q5 *steady*. Q6 no dependants. Q7 prayers, Qur'an. Q8 "my Islamic society's timetable, method not known" → cautious windows; Asr rule: later. Q9 prayer room, times "I'll confirm when I go". Q10 Qur'an: usual 20 minutes, minimum 5. Q11 *late-night use*, wind-down reminder at 23:30. **Known (Told):** all the above. **Assumed:** none shown as fact. **Unknown:** the society's method, prayer-room times, real sleep pattern, what she does late at night.

**Initial plan (Draft).** Cautious windows; lectures as hard blocks; Qur'an proposed *after Fajr* as a cue (needs her approval, T1) with a 5-minute floor; the 23:30 reminder she asked for; evenings left empty. Nothing is placed in free time.

**Ordinary day.** Fajr reminder (chosen). Qur'an after Fajr, 10 minutes (between minimum and usual, no comment). Lectures 09:00 to 13:00. Zuhr falls after lectures with a long slack, so no message (R1, R6). The later Asr window opens late afternoon; one reminder near the end of the window. Evening open. 23:30 reminder. Messages used: three of her chosen budget.

**Unexpected change.** A lab moves to Thursday 16:30 to 19:30, overlapping most of the later Asr window and Maghrib. Step 0 shows slack: "Asr window under your chosen rule: 15:45 to 17:05. The lab starts at 16:30: 45 minutes before it." Step 3: the system names the options she controls (pray before the lab, ask the lab supervisor about a short break for Maghrib, other) and does not suggest missing a window. It asks only, "How would you like to handle Maghrib during the lab?" She says she will ask her TA. The system offers a drafted message only if she wants one.

**First-week adaptation.** *Reasonable to learn:* she moved Qur'an to after Maghrib three times (a pattern across two weeks is needed, so it is asked as a question, T2); she dismissed the Fajr reminder twice in a row. The system asks "Stop these?" She says no, move it 15 minutes later. *Must not assume:* that she is skipping Fajr, that she was unwell on certain days, that late-night phone use is the cause of late sleep. A "pause religious reminders" switch is used on several days without explanation. Nothing is logged about why.

**Failure and uncertainty.** Day 4: the system links her late nights to "phone use," because she ticked that in Q11, and offers a stricter wind-down. She replies she is writing her thesis. The system had overread one tick: a stated concern about late-night use is not evidence of distraction. Recovery: it states what the wind-down was based on, says it does not know what she does at night, and asks whether to retire it or change it. She retires it. It adds no inference about her phone.

### Simulation 2: Yusuf, 34, salaried office worker (hypothetical)

Fixed hours 09:00 to 17:30, 50-minute train commute, hybrid two days a week, spouse at home, no children. Follows his local mosque's timetable. Wants Jumu'ah at the mosque near the office, Maghrib or Isha at the mosque on two evenings, and some exercise.

**First use.** Q1 *Active partner*. Q3 fixed hours; commute 50 to 60 minutes. Q4 office hours and train hard, exercise flexible. Q5 steady. Q6 household duties; evening meal at 19:30 fixed. Q7 prayers, Jumu'ah, Qur'an, voluntary prayer. Q8 "my local mosque's timetable"; Asr: follow the mosque. Q9 mosque for Jumu'ah and two evenings; he types the iqama times; confirmed this week. Q10 Qur'an 15 minutes on the train; minimum 3. Q11 quiet during work blocks. **Unknown:** whether his employer will release him on Fridays, how often hybrid days change, when the mosque changes its timetable.

**Initial plan.** Zuhr inside the lunch break. Friday: "Mosque iqama 13:15 (your entry, confirmed 2 days ago). Your break starts 13:00. Walk: 10 minutes (your estimate)." Two evening slots tagged *Told*. Exercise offered as a flexible slot, not scheduled.

**Ordinary day.** Fajr at home, no message. Qur'an on the train. Zuhr at the desk. Active partner use: one message at 15:50, "Asr window ends 16:40; your 15:30 meeting ends 16:15." That is useful information, not exhortation. Tuesday evening: a leave-by message for the mosque (confirmed time plus his own travel estimate). Home for dinner at 19:30, which is why the leave-by exists.

**Unexpected change.** The employer schedules an all-hands at 13:00 on Friday (see the illustration in 4.3). The system lays out the overlap, notes that positions on excuses for Jumu'ah differ and that employment terms and local law are outside its knowledge, and does not say which should win. It offers two optional tools: a short summary to take to his scholar and a drafted message to his manager. He asks his manager whether the meeting can start at 14:00. The system does not claim to know whether that is allowed.

**First-week adaptation.** *Learns:* he dismisses the Asr reminder three times; asked whether to stop, he keeps it but moves it. His evening exercise becomes a walk. *Must not assume:* that his stated two evenings should be three, that he attends every Maghrib, or that dismissed items mean low commitment.

**Failure and uncertainty.** The mosque changed its iqama time without notice. The leave-by message was based on a time last confirmed nine days earlier. He arrives after the prayer begins. The system could not have known. Fix: the "last confirmed" date was already visible; it now also asks "Still right?" before sending a leave-by if the confirmation is older than two weeks \[Hyp\]. It does not claim to verify mosque schedules (R10).

### Simulation 3: Hana, 31, parent with significant caregiving (hypothetical)

Three children (6, 3, 8 months), nursing the baby, partner working away on weekdays. Prays at home. Follows her country's official timetable.

**First use.** Q1 *Quiet planner*. Q3 *Mostly caregiving*; least interruptible: none; most interrupted: late afternoon. Q5 *unpredictable*, "plan around that." Q6 children, fixed: school run 08:15 and 15:00; baby's feeds unpredictable. Q7 prayers, Qur'an, dhikr. Q8 country authority; Asr: follow the authority. Q9 *doesn't apply now*. Q10 dhikr during feeds; Qur'an usual 10 minutes, minimum "listening while cooking." Q11 silent during school run and bedtime. **Unknown:** nap pattern, who else can help, anything about her health.

**Initial plan.** Windows, not times. No pre-dawn or late-night items (R9). Minimum sizes shown first. A cue "after the 15:00 snack" is proposed and she accepts it. Two evening blocks marked *leave empty*.

**Ordinary day.** 05:00 baby wakes; no messages (Quiet planner). 06:10 she opens the app to see when the Fajr window ends; it shows the end under her authority's timetable. Qur'an listening during cooking counts as her minimum. The prayer windows are long; the system does not narrow them. Evening: the system is silent. One pull of information; zero pushes. The day contains no system decisions except those it did not make.

**Unexpected change.** A child has a fever and school is closed. "Something came up" starts disruption mode (R15): proactive messages freeze, only windows and hard blocks are shown, voluntary items collapse to minimum. She asks, "Can I combine prayers, I'm exhausted?" This is T3. She asked directly, so R2 allows an answer: it names the positions (Muslim 705 ✔ report; the Hanafi reading versus broader schools, 2.3), notes that this is a juristic disagreement, and offers a question to bring to a scholar. It does not say yes or no.

**First-week adaptation.** *Learns:* real nap windows. *Must not assume:* that she has no time for anything, that she wants mosque or community items, that her partner helps, that her nursing status determines Ramadan fasting. A Ramadan question would be asked three weeks before (expected early in 2027; the date itself depends on method). Fasting while nursing is referred to a clinician and a scholar.

**Failure and uncertainty.** She asks about how to pray when holding the baby and when clothing may be soiled. The system's reviewed content has nothing on purity details of that kind. It says so, offers a summary of the question to take to a scholar, and does not improvise. A second failure: on day 6 her partner is home and she wants more time. The system had been holding minimum sizes (R16) and had not offered to expand. She taps "back to usual." The system learns only that the control exists, not why.

### Simulation 4: Omar, 45, business owner (hypothetical)

Owns a small logistics firm, six staff, constant calls. Spouse and two teenagers. Follows the Ja'fari tradition and a method from his own scholarly authority.

**First use.** Q1 *Gentle*. Q3 *Variable*: reliably his are family dinner and Sunday morning. Q4 deliveries hard. Q5 steady but short (six hours). Q6 household; Sunday lunch fixed. Q7 prayers, Qur'an. Q8: he selects *other* and names his authority's method. **The system cannot compute windows under that method and has no scholar-reviewed content for his tradition.** It says so plainly and offers to use times he enters manually.

**Initial plan.** Protected anchors only: sleep, family dinner, Sunday morning, the prayer times he enters, and Qur'an with a 5-minute floor. No clock-time plan for the rest.

**Ordinary day.** Mostly silent. Gentle reminders at the windows he entered. System decisions: none except preserving what he marked as protected.

**Unexpected change.** A supplier truck breaks down at 14:00 and the crisis lasts four hours. Disruption mode (R15). Voluntary items collapse. At 18:30 the system asks once, "Dinner at 19:30: keep it or move it?" He moves it. T2 approval. No catch-up scheduled.

**First-week adaptation.** *Learns:* Tuesdays and Thursdays are chaotic (offered as a confirmed pattern, not applied silently). Protected anchors survive better than clock times, which supports the anchor design for variable schedules \[Hyp for this domain\]. *Must not assume:* that long hours mean he neglects family, or that he wants the system to block work. When he sets a plan with four hours of sleep, R18 states concern once with the rights principle and offers a lighter plan. He declines. The system does not repeat itself.

**Failure and uncertainty.** The framework's religious layer rests on sources that are predominantly Sunni, and no scholar from his tradition has reviewed any of it (Deliverable 6). For Omar the system must withhold concession and disputed-matter content it cannot stand behind, and be explicit that this is a coverage gap, not a statement about his practice. It continues to plan his life around the times he provides. This case is a real constraint on Decision 1.

### Simulation 5: Fatima, 29, night-shift hospital nurse (hypothetical)

Rotating 12-hour night shifts, three or four per week, roster published weekly with occasional short-notice changes. Follows the majority Asr rule and an ISNA-style calculation. Prays at work in a quiet room. Wants Qur'an and voluntary fasting.

**First use.** Q1 *Gentle*. Q3 *Shifts*: weekly roster, sometimes short notice; necessarily asleep roughly late morning to afternoon after a night. Q4 shifts and sleep hard. Q5 *depends on shifts*. Q6 none. Q7 prayers, Qur'an, voluntary fasting. Q8 ISNA-style. Q9 alone. Q10 Qur'an 10 minutes, minimum "listening on the way home"; voluntary fasting Mondays and Thursdays. R18 triggers: heavy fasting during night weeks. The system says once that it can be demanding and offers fasting only on days off; she chooses days off. Q11 quiet during sleep. **Unknown:** her break times, how reliably she can pray at work.

**Initial plan.** Per-roster blocks. Her "day" begins when she wakes. Optional morning items hidden after nights. Zuhr and Asr fall inside protected sleep. The system asks, "Wake inside the window, or leave sleep protected?" and notes scholars discuss sleeping through a prayer (Muslim 681 ○; Bukhari 597 ○). She sets one alarm to pray Zuhr before the window ends.

**Ordinary day (night-shift day).** Wake mid-afternoon; Zuhr by alarm; Maghrib before shift; Isha at the shift break; Fajr near the end of shift. The Isha window needs care: under her stated method the window ends around midnight, while her break is after that. The system shows the method's boundary and says that scholars distinguish a preferred time from a wider time of necessity, with sources, and refers (T3). It does not move her break or her prayer.

**Unexpected change.** The roster flips with 24 hours' notice. R5: the system offers to rebuild sleep anchors, shows the diff, and asks approval. The Monday fast now falls on a shift day: it asks whether to keep or move it, and does not decide.

**First-week adaptation.** *Learns:* she sleeps in two blocks rather than one (told, by correction). *Must not assume:* where she can pray at work, that she wants to combine prayers, that she fasts when scheduled, or anything about her health.

**Failure and uncertainty.** After midnight the system labelled the next calendar date's Fajr instead of the one ending her shift. She noticed. The system admits the error, shows both candidate Fajr windows, and asks which day boundary she wants. For any shift crossing midnight it now displays both. The error shows how a calendar-date assumption can quietly produce a wrong religious statement.

### Simulation 6: Idris, 52, wheelchair user working remotely (hypothetical)

Translator working from home, lives alone. Prays seated. Wudu takes about 15 minutes. Needs step-free access. Energy is best in the morning. Mentioned an out-of-town conference next month (deferred to Stage 2).

**First use.** Q1 *Quiet planner*. Q3 *Variable*; mornings reliably his. Q5 steady. Functional needs (3.5): prays seated, wudu 15 minutes, step-free access, rests between tasks. No reason is requested. Q7 prayers, Jumu'ah, Qur'an, study circle. Q8 his mosque's timetable. Q9 Jumu'ah at a named mosque with a ramp he confirmed; Q10 Qur'an 20 minutes in the morning, minimum 5. **Unknown:** other accessible places, fatigue patterns, transport reliability.

**Initial plan.** Each prayer carries a "setup time" (wudu plus transfer) so the system shows "begin by" times rather than only the window end. Jumu'ah leave-by combines his own travel and transfer estimates. Mornings protected for work and Qur'an.

**Ordinary day.** Qur'an and work in the morning. Zuhr "begin by" at 15 minutes before the window's end, shown only if he asked for reminders (he did not; he glances at it). Rest in the afternoon, left empty. Evening quiet.

**Unexpected change.** The mosque's ramp is closed for repairs on Friday. He enters the note. The system does not assume another mosque is accessible. It asks, "Is there another place you know is accessible?" He names one 14 km away with a lift. It computes leave-by from his own travel and transfer estimates. It also notes (R8, T3) that scholars discuss excuses for Jumu'ah in situations like this, names the positions, and refers. It does not tell him he is excused or not.

**First-week adaptation.** *Learns:* wudu takes 12 minutes on good days and 25 on tired days. He adds a "tired day" switch (no diagnosis) that scales buffers and shrinks the plan to minimum. *Must not assume:* that his disability excuses him from anything, that fatigue indicates a particular condition, that he cannot travel, or that any accessibility claim found elsewhere is true.

**Failure and uncertainty.** The second mosque's "lift" came from a friend's tip, entered as unconfirmed. The leave-by message displayed "accessibility: not confirmed." He arrives and finds the accessible toilet out of service. The system can only report what he told it. It does not verify buildings. Recovery: it records "lift: confirmed; accessible toilet: out of service, as of today," and does not suggest that mosque for Jumu'ah again unless he asks.

### 5.7 What the six cases show

- **Silence did most of the work** in four of six cases. Whether that helps real people is **\[Hyp\]**.
- **Most conflicts were opportunity problems** (roster, lab, meeting, ramp), not motivation problems. Information and reorganisation addressed them; nudging would not have.
- **Every failure came from the system holding a fact with more confidence than it deserved** (a tick read as a cause, a stale mosque time, a wrong calendar date, an unconfirmed ramp) or from a content gap (a tradition with no reviewed sources, a purity question). The confidence labels, *last confirmed* dates and the willingness to say "I don't know" are not cosmetic. They are the recovery mechanism.
- **The hardest design cases are not exotic.** They are Isha's end time, Jumu'ah versus work, combining prayers, and fasting with other responsibilities. All four are T3 matters where the system's job is to inform and refer.

## 6. Deliverable 5: Rules and Boundaries

### 6.1 The system must

- Show prayer windows only under the person's chosen method, or as a labelled **cautious window** when the method is unknown.
- Tag every statement by layer (religious content, the person's rulebook, life planning) and by confidence (*Told, Observed, Assumed, Unknown*).
- Treat the person's declared rulebook as the basis for planning and show who said what ("per your scholar").
- Default to silence, and honour the assistance level (Q1) and quiet times (Q11).
- Offer a minimum version of every voluntary item and treat shrinking as normal.
- Present disputed matters as disputed, with named positions and sources, and refer.
- Treat sleep and family blocks as hard when the person says they are.
- Show what it knows, assumes and does not know, with one-tap correction.
- Let the person pause, hide or remove any layer, or leave, without retention prompts.
- Say "I don't know" when reviewed content is missing and show where the gap is.
- Voice a concern once when a plan appears harmful, then defer.
- Show "last confirmed" next to mosque times and any Volatile fact.
- Keep working with no religious layer on.

### 6.2 The system must never

- **Issue or imply a ruling.** No "permissible," "forbidden" or "required for you" on any disputed matter, and no use of obligation as pressure on someone who has not opted in.
- **Silently choose** a calculation method, school, high-latitude rule or concession.
- **Infer or store** devotion level, observance, sect, cycle, pregnancy, health, disability, family conflict or any item in 3.6.
- **Score, rank, streak, badge, compare or gamify** religious acts, or show social feeds of worship.
- **Count or display missed prayers or fasts**, or compute any "debt."
- **Use guilt, fear, shame or urgency** ("you missed") in religious messaging. Use autonomy-supportive wording.
- **Suggest combining, skipping or delaying a prayer** as a scheduling convenience.
- **Fill free time** or optimise it without a request. Escalate reminders because they were ignored.
- **Monitor passively:** phone content, app usage, location trails, microphone, contacts.
- **Present a historical example** (Salman and Abu al-Darda', al-Ghazali's litanies, the Companions' work and study rotation) as a universal timetable, or a weakly graded narration as established.
- **Move a hard constraint** or move a prayer in the plan without the person's approval.
- **Advise on medical questions:** fasting while ill, pregnancy, medication, exhaustion.
- **Claim effectiveness** it has not demonstrated ("build a habit in 21 days," "you'll feel more balanced").

### 6.3 What requires the person's approval

- **T2 (plan-structural):** changing a sleep window; adding a commitment; adopting an observed pattern; any reorganisation; changing quiet hours; enabling a new reminder type; adding or changing a mosque; storing any sensitive item; changing method.
- **T3 (religiously consequential; requires the person's own declared position):** any plan that depends on a concession; any plan that depends on a disputed matter; Jumu'ah arrangements; Ramadan mode and any fasting plan.

### 6.4 What must remain subject to human or qualified scholarly judgement

- What is obligatory in a person's circumstances; whether a concession applies; congregation and Jumu'ah excuses; combining prayers; fasting with illness, pregnancy or nursing; making up missed or deliberately delayed prayers and fasts; purity questions; the details of women-specific rulings.
- Conflicts between the rights of different people, including parents, spouse and children.
- Employer duty versus prayer windows, and any legal question.
- Medical decisions, and anything involving abuse, crisis or risk to life. The system does not continue planning in those situations. It points to emergency or trusted human help.

## 7. Deliverable 6: Foundation Readiness Assessment

### 7.1 Decisions supported by sufficiently strong evidence

These are source-level or finding-level, not system-level.

- **Plural rights as the organising principle** (Bukhari 1968 ✔, 6139 ✔). Strongly sourced. The *application* (no ratios, no ranking) is a limit of the source, not a gap.
- **Obligations fixed in core and flexible in mode; voluntary worship scalable.** Supported across the Qur'an and the Sahih collections (Q 4:103, 2:185, 73:20 ○; Bukhari 39, 1117 ○; Muslim 705 ✔).
- **Legitimate scholarly disagreement exists on prayer-time calculation and on combining.** Documented (ECFR ✔, Dar al-Ifta Jordan ✔, Muslim 705 ✔, Bukhari 543 ✔). The Companions also differed in how to apply a prophetic instruction about Asr, and the Prophet ﷺ did not rebuke either group (Bukhari 946 ○, Muslim 1770 ○): support for treating some differences as legitimate rather than errors.
- **If-then planning improves goal attainment on average** (d = 0.65 over 94 tests; Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 ✔), within the limits in 4.8.
- **Habit formation is slow and highly variable** (Lally et al. 2010 ✔).
- **JITAI is a well-specified framework** (Nahum-Shani et al. 2018 ✔), and a 2025 review documents under-implemented adaptivity and receptivity ✔.

### 7.2 Reasonable provisional assumptions

These are \[Rec\] or \[Hyp\] and are not proven. Each is a candidate for the tests in 7.5.

- Layer separation (4.1) and confidence labels.
- Silence as the default; reminder ceiling of three per day; stop-after-two-dismissals; observed pattern needs three occurrences over two weeks.
- Two-size commitments (usual and hard-week minimum).
- Cue anchoring to prayer events rather than clock times.
- The "cautious window" fallback (intersection of common methods).
- No scores, no streaks, no missed-prayer counts.
- Eleven prompts to a first draft plan.
- Disruption mode and its steps.

### 7.3 Decisions requiring qualified scholarly review

- **The cautious-window rule.** Whether using the latest start and earliest end across common methods is acceptable to scholars of the relevant schools, and whether it ever misleads (for example, around Ramadan imsak or at high latitudes).
- **All Layer R content:** wording of positions, tier assignments, which items count as "well-established and broadly shared."
- **Coverage of traditions.** Ja'fari, Zaydi and Ibadi scholars must review before Decision 1 can be honoured. Today, the sources used here do not cover them.
- **Women-specific items** (mosque attendance, fasting permission, menstrual and postnatal rulings) require review by qualified female and male scholars.
- **Concession presentation** (travel, illness, combining): what may be offered as information, and how the referral wording avoids reading as a ruling.
- **Hadith numbering and grading** for every ○ item in this document.
- **The Isha end-time presentation** (preferred time versus necessity).

### 7.4 Decisions requiring empirical testing

- Whether **onboarding length** and each sensitive question lowers completion or plan quality.
- Whether first-draft plans match real days, and how much declared routines diverge from observed ones for each persona group.
- Whether **silence-first** improves or worsens wellbeing and obligation-fulfilment compared with reminders.
- Whether reminder ceilings and dismissal thresholds are correct.
- Whether people **understand layer tags** and do not read planning suggestions as rulings.
- Whether **two-size commitments** reduce discouragement.
- Whether **cue-anchoring to prayers** outperforms clock times (hypothesis).
- Whether prompting changes worship-adjacent or family behaviour at all (transfer from health domains is unproven).
- **Harm:** scrupulosity, guilt, family conflict, over-reliance on the system for religious judgements, privacy discomfort.

### 7.5 What would prove the foundation sound, and what would remove or reject a mechanism

Each mechanism needs independent evidence of its own type. Passing one type does not validate the others.

**1. Religious-source verification (scholars).**

- *Method:* a panel spanning schools and traditions, including women scholars and an astronomer for prayer-time computation. For each Layer R item: source, number, grading, tier, positions mapped, wording tested for neutrality; independent double review; versioned sign-off.
- *Remove or simplify if:* reviewers cannot agree on neutral wording or tier, or the item rests on a disputed grading. Move to "refer only" or remove.

**2. Observational research into everyday behaviour.**

- *Method:* diary or experience-sampling over two to four weeks, consented, with participants across the six archetypes and beyond; compare onboarding answers with observed routines.
- *Remove or simplify if:* an onboarding item does not predict observed routines better than a default, or declared routines mispredict real blocks for a persona group; move to learn-from-corrections.

**3. Validation of onboarding questions.**

- *Method:* cognitive interviewing and think-aloud, test-retest reliability, and an **ablation test**: build plans with and without each question and have blinded raters and participants judge fit. Track completion and skip rates by sensitivity.
- *Remove, demote to Stage 2, or reject if:* plan quality is unchanged within a pre-set margin; or a sensitive item is frequently skipped with no loss of quality.

**4. Comprehension and usability.**

- *Method:* ask participants to state what the system knows, assumes and does not know; to distinguish a ruling from a plan from a scholarly note; to correct a wrong assumption.
- *Remove or simplify if:* misattribution of planning suggestions as rulings stays above a pre-set threshold after redesign \[Hyp: 10 percent\], or correction takes more than a few steps.

**5. Practical feasibility.**

- *Method:* check computed windows against reference timetables and scholars across latitudes; measure the effort needed to keep user-confirmed mosque times current; check whether disruption mode can be triggered reliably.
- *Remove if:* congregation "leave-by" suggestions are mostly built on stale data, or window computation cannot be signed off for a method; reduce to information only.

**6. Comparison with simple planning methods.**

- *Method:* randomised or crossover comparison of (a) a paper or calendar planner, (b) calendar plus a prayer-time source, (c) the full system, with human coaching or scholar Q&A as an optional fourth arm. Pre-registered primary outcome: balance satisfaction across domains the person weights themselves, with a wellbeing measure such as WHO-5 ○. Secondary: sleep, role-conflict episodes, time pressure. Follow-up at eight to twelve weeks, since habit timescales are long. **No religiosity score** is used as an outcome.
- *Reject or collapse to the simpler arm if:* (c) does not beat (b) on the primary outcome.

**7. Measurement of unintended harm.**

- *Method:* ethics-reviewed qualitative interviews and validated measures where appropriate (for example the Penn Inventory of Scrupulosity, Abramowitz et al. 2002 ○), with a defined adverse-event process. Watch for guilt, scrupulosity, family conflict, sleep loss from over-ambitious plans, over-reliance on the system for religious questions and perceived surveillance.
- *Remove or redesign if:* a mechanism is associated with increased distress or ruling-attribution, even if satisfaction is high.

**8. Controlled evaluation of personalised intervention effectiveness.**

- *Method:* micro-randomised trials of specific decision rules (for example a window-closing reminder versus none; a two-size suggestion versus one size), always including a **no-prompt arm** (Klasnja et al. 2015 ○); N-of-1 designs for individuals; outcomes the person chose.
- *Default to silence for a rule if:* prompts do not beat no-prompt on the person's own target over the pre-set period.

**9. Feedback is not evidence.** Likes, ratings, retention and session counts are not outcomes (Decision 5). Religious social desirability may inflate satisfaction. Satisfaction is measured separately from effect.

**What this document does not claim.** That the underlying principles are supported does not mean the complete system is validated. Nothing here has been tested.

### 7.6 Contradictions and unresolved dependencies

1. **"No silent defaults" versus "minimal questions."** Disputed matters need asking, but asking costs. Provisional resolution: ask when needed, use the cautious window otherwise. Depends on scholarly review (7.3).
2. **"Verified mosque schedules" versus "no unverifiable data."** No verifier exists. Provisional: user-confirmed only. Depends on testing whether the confirmation burden is tolerable.
3. **"Universal" versus "broadly shared."** Coverage today is predominantly Sunni. Depends on the scholarly panel's composition.
4. **"Not a prayer app" versus prayer windows as the main structural anchor.** Prayer times are scaffolding for planning, not a tracker. The distinction must hold in every feature.
5. **Silence-first versus obligations.** The Qur'an says reminder benefits the believers (Q 51:55 ○), and silence may let an intended obligation lapse. Resolution depends on Q1 and honest offering of reminders at the start, and on test 8.
6. **Individual control versus harm prevention.** The person can request an unhealthy plan. Provisional: voice concern once (R18), then defer; refer for serious risk.
7. **Success as balance versus measurement.** Balance is user-defined, which makes it hard to compare across people. Depends on test 6's design.
8. **A rulebook the person owns versus the person's mistaken understanding.** If a person's rulebook contains an error, the system cannot correct it without issuing rulings. Provisional: it can present named positions on request and refer.

### 7.7 Questions that must be answered before the foundation can be finalised

1. Which scholars (schools, traditions, genders) sign off Layer R, and what is the review process?
2. Is the cautious-window rule acceptable, and for which prayers?
3. Which concession and disputed-matter content, if any, ships in the first version?
4. How will Ja'fari, Zaydi and Ibadi traditions be covered, or explicitly excluded with a clear statement?
5. What is the data-never list (3.6), and has a legal review of special-category data been completed?
6. What assistance levels exist and what is the reminder ceiling per level?
7. How is "balance" shown to the person without becoming a score?
8. What does "minimum size" mean for each voluntary category, and who defines it?
9. How is mosque and congregation information entered and kept current without implying verification?
10. What is the pre-registered comparison against a calendar plus prayer-time source?

### 7.8 Questions that can safely be deferred

- Detailed Ramadan planning beyond the three-week prompt and fasting suitability referral.
- Hajj and Umrah planning.
- Zakat and financial planning.
- Marriage, parenting guidance and conflict resolution content.
- Halal and haram analysis of work.
- Community, service and charity coordination beyond the person's own planning.
- Any shared or multi-person features (family planning, groups). They carry high riya' and privacy risk and need separate research.
- Languages, regions and calendars beyond those needed for the first scholarly panel.

### 7.9 The ten most important concrete decisions

1. **Adopt the three-layer separation** (religious content, personal rulebook, life planning) with the rule that no layer is inferred from another and no disputed matter is defaulted silently.
2. **Fix the constraint classes** (fixed religious windows, hard practical, soft commitments, preferences, capacity) and the conflict procedure in 4.3: the system presents and the person decides.
3. **Set the religious scope of the first version** in writing: what is included (windows under a chosen method, information on named positions with referral) and what is excluded (rulings on combining for work, missed-prayer computation, women-specific disputed rulings, any tradition not yet reviewed).
4. **Constitute the scholarly review panel** and process, including traditions beyond the Sunni schools and women scholars, with sign-off required before any Layer R content ships.
5. **Define the assistance levels and the silence policy:** default quiet, a user-set reminder ceiling, dismissal handling, long-absence behaviour.
6. **Fix the onboarding budget:** the eleven prompts in 3.3, the "unknown" fallbacks (including the cautious window), and the rule that everything else is asked at the moment it changes a decision.
7. **Fix the data boundaries:** the never-infer and never-collect lists (3.6), data minimisation, and legal review of religion and health data.
8. **Decide how balance is shown:** user-weighted domains, no devotion score, no streaks, no counts of missed obligations; define what the person sees instead.
9. **Decide the voluntary-commitment mechanism:** two sizes (usual and hard-week minimum), no failure state, and the rule for restoring usual size.
10. **Set the validation gate:** the pre-registered comparison against a calendar plus prayer-time source, the unintended-harm protocol, and the removal criteria in 7.5, agreed before detailed product behaviour is specified.
