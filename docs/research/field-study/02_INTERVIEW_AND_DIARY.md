# Recent-event interview, observation and optional short diary

**Stage A only.** Conduct before exposing participants to the planned interface or synthetic comparisons. No participant research is claimed complete.

## Interview structure (roughly 45–60 minutes)

**Opening:** Read approved consent, clarify that good existing habits and *no need for an app* are welcome outcomes. Request permission *each time* before viewing any app or paper. Do not photograph or export the participant's actual data by default.

**Recent event, last ~14 days when possible:**

1. “Think of a recent time two appointments, responsibilities or preferred activities competed for time. What happened?” (If none, **record no-conflict**, do not invent one.)
2. “What was fixed, flexible or unknown when you first noticed it?”
3. “What information did you consult, and which calendar, reminder, paper sheet or person helped?”
4. “Which action could you take yourself, and which change needed another person's permission?”
5. “What did you actually do? Did it work? What still felt difficult?”
6. “Has a similar difficulty happened more than once recently? How do ordinary days go smoothly?”
7. “Would a correctly configured ordinary calendar, a printable worksheet or a simple conversation have solved it?”
8. “What additional information would a proposed tool have needed? Would maintaining it have been worth the work?”
9. “Which details would you **not** share with an app, a researcher or a cloud service?” (No request to disclose those details.)
10. “Would you be comfortable with us checking a short, anonymous reconstruction of the sequence for factual accuracy?”

**Participant-led workflow:** With separate permission, observe only navigation steps needed to solve the chosen task; ask the participant to obscure names, times and other personal content. If direct viewing is unsafe or refused, take a verbal reconstruction. Record observation separately from researcher inference.

**Closing:** Invite factual corrections, ask about any discomfort or assumptions made by the interviewer, explain optional diary and rights to withdrawal, and reiterate that declining a proposed app is valuable evidence.

## Episode worksheet — PRIVATE; blank structure

Use a random participant ID managed separately from the contact key. Never place completed worksheets in the public repository.

| Field | Entry guidance |
|---|---|
| Anonymous-local ID / method | Random local ID; interview date can be kept privately |
| Event status | Real recalled / real observed with consent / synthetic reconstruction / no recent event |
| Relative recency | e.g. “past two weeks”; avoid exact identifiable dates |
| Actual competing constraints | Coarse descriptions: fixed, flexible, other person's permission, unknown |
| Knowledge at the time | What was known vs learned later |
| Baseline tools and human workarounds | Participant's words, not a preselected app category |
| Actions feasible without others' permission | Concrete options or “none” |
| Outcome and consequence | How the participant describes impact; do not infer missed religious duty |
| Burden | Setup, switching/checking, correction, communication; approximate and participant-rated |
| Privacy refusals and safety flags | Skip sensitive specifics; private escalation if necessary |
| Evidence classification | Observed, recalled, reported, hypothetical, or researcher inference |
| Negative/disconfirming observation | Effective baseline, no problem, refused diary, or structural barrier |
| Participant correction | Clarification to factual sequence, not proof the intervention is useful |

## Optional seven-day diary — opt-in separately

No push messages by default and **at most one voluntary short note per day**. A paper sheet or locally saved private blank form is acceptable. “Not reported” is missing, not zero conflict or spiritual failure.

| Prompt | Acceptable response |
|---|---|
| Did a meaningful schedule conflict arise? | Yes / No / Prefer not to say / No entry |
| Broad constraints | Fixed / Flexible / Another person's approval / Unknown |
| Broad time period | Morning / Afternoon / Evening / Prefer not to say |
| What did you actually try? | A few non-identifying words, or skip |
| Existing tools helped? | Adequately / Partially / No / Not applicable / Skip |
| Was any outcome actionable? | Could adjust / Required another person / Structural / Unknown |
| Burden or unwanted pressure | Optional coarse response |
| No-event day | Explicit “No” is a legitimate result |

The participant may hand back no entries, refuse or withdraw with no consequence. Do not infer religious practice, diagnosis or personal identity from silence. At follow-up, confirm meaning of selected entries; do not pressure people to reveal the event's details.

## Distinguish these outcomes

- **Informational:** A time, validity or source was unknown; showing accurate information might change a decision.
- **Coordinational/actionable:** An actual user-controlled or consensually negotiable adjustment exists, but existing workflow made it difficult to identify/evaluate.
- **Structural:** No safe feasible user-controlled action; the blocker is another person's consent, employer/institution rule, required care or lack of resource. A planner cannot solve it by asserting a change.
- **Baseline adequate:** Existing approach already handles it acceptably.
- **No conflict / unclear:** Do not promote hypothetical frustration into “unmet need.”

Assign multiple categories only when justified by the episode. Preserve dissent and participants' own interpretation. A person may be satisfied even when researchers think their tools could be optimized.
