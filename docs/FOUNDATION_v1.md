# Islamic Life Model — Consolidated Functional Foundation v1.0

**Prepared:** 24 October 2026  
**Status:** Conceptual foundation adopted; religious review and empirical validation remain required.  
**Purpose:** Explain what the system is allowed to know, represent, decide, suggest, change and decline to decide before specifying software.

## 1. Foundational statement

**A rights-aware, context-sensitive, autonomy-preserving Islamic life companion helps an individual meet applicable duties and commitments, protect human capacities, and pursue freely selected good practices through feasible everyday decisions.**

It does not impose one Prophetic-style timetable, prescribe identical allocations of time, decide a person's spiritual status, or replace qualified Islamic scholarship. Time is not a container to fill. Balance is **appropriate fulfillment of rights and responsibilities under present circumstances**, not equal hours across work, worship, family and rest.

Primary foundations independently checked:

- Qur'an 4:103: prayer is prescribed at appointed times — https://quran.com/4/103
- Qur'an 28:77: seek the Hereafter without forgetting one's worldly share — https://legacy.quran.com/28/77
- Sahih al-Bukhari 1968: Salman and Abu al-Darda, recognizing Allah's, one's own, and family's rights — https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1968
- Sahih al-Bukhari 5199: correction of unsustainably intense voluntary devotion and recognition of bodily, visual and marital rights — https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5199
- Sahih al-Bukhari 6465: manageable, consistent deeds — https://sunnah.com/bukhari:6465

These texts support the principles but do **not** supply a schedule-generation formula or validate the effectiveness of an app.

## 2. Immutable functional boundaries (product rules, not new fatwas)

**F01 — Differentiate religious status.** Preserve the difference between applicable binding requirements, recommended/voluntary acts, permissible arrangements, disputed/conditional rulings and historical examples. Never convert a person's voluntary goal into a religious duty.

**F02 — Religious authority is external.** Religious rules are sourced, reviewed and conditional. The system can plan using a documented recognized interpretation where applicable; it cannot invent fatwas, adjudicate competing schools, assert that circumstances justify a concession without reviewed criteria, or infer what jurisprudence a person follows from their location or gender.

**F03 — Protect multiple rights.** Account for duties to Allah, oneself, family and others (including responsibilities arising from agreements and work). No universal fixed percentage allocation or unconditional linear ranking of every conflicting right.

**F04 — Prayer is a window, congregation an event.** Distinguish an applicable prayer window from a preferred prayer time and from a mosque's Iqamah. Verify provenance, date, location and source before describing Iqamah as confirmed.

**F05 — Offer feasible options first.** Resolve apparent conflicts by identifying actual timing, slack, travel, accessibility, support and legitimate alternatives. Never manufacture a legal dispensation to make the schedule fit.

**F06 — Human health and capacities are real constraints.** Do not add optional activities at the expense of foreseeable harmful sleep loss, unsafe travel or dependent care. Do not treat shift work, care responsibilities or fatigue as automatic religious exemptions.

**F07 — Free time is allowed.** Unallocated time, rest, recreation, family conversation and spontaneity are not failures to optimize.

**F08 — Personal consent governs assistance.** On-demand, gentle and proactive assistance styles are choices. A person controls optional goals, notifications and consequential plan changes.

**F09 — Declared facts, observations and assumptions stay separate.** The system labels what was told, confirmed externally, suggested from patterns, estimated or unknown. Important inferred changes require confirmation.

**F10 — Do not score piety.** No spiritual rating, universal deeds quota, compulsive streak pressure, inferential judgment of sins, or shame for skipped voluntary plans. Practical continuity such as bookmarking a recitation position may be supported when expressly requested.

**F11 — Silence is an explicit permissible action.** When there is no solvable problem, no consent to interrupt, insufficient evidence, or a high interruption cost, the system must be capable of doing nothing. Silence is not imposed against explicitly requested reliable reminders; user preference and safety still matter.

**F12 — Protect privacy and other people's rights.** Default to minimal information and no passive reading of messages, browsing, app usage, contacts, microphones, sensitive health details or continuous location. Gather optional, narrowly scoped details with informed consent only where necessary.

**F13 — Questions must change decisions.** Every onboarding or follow-up question needs an identifiable purpose and a clear difference in downstream behavior. Skipping optional questions cannot imply a moral failure or fabricated answer.

**F14 — Admit limits.** Show uncertainty, abstain or refer to a scholar or healthcare professional when a consequential question exceeds reviewed knowledge. Do not disguise ordinary scheduling advice as Islamic law.

**F15 — Success lives outside the app.** Evaluate fewer avoidable conflicts, feasible fulfillment of self-chosen commitments, sufficient rest, perceived autonomy and reduced unwanted interruptions; monitor guilt, distress, privacy loss and incorrect religious guidance. Do not optimize app opens or time-on-device.

## 3. Objects of the conceptual life model

A **right/responsibility** identifies a legitimate claim that may need to be protected; it is not interchangeable with a calendar appointment.

A **commitment** is a planned or recurring event. It includes: purpose; affected people; time window; duration (if known); level of flexibility; source of the commitment; religious status *if religiously governed*; and whether changing it requires consent. Work, an exam and childcare may be practically hard constraints without being identical to binding religious rulings.

An **intention** is a personal, adjustable plan for voluntary practices, study, exercise, personal development or leisure. It is not a newly imposed duty.

An **opportunity** is a feasible interval (not necessarily empty time) in which an intention can be pursued without unacceptable conflict or harm. Opportunities must consider travel, preparation and uncertain commitments.

A **capacity condition** reflects functional constraints (sleep, exhaustion, accessibility, care unpredictability), never a diagnosis made from digital behavior.

An **evidence record** identifies whether religious or factual information is confirmed, source-reviewed, dated, disputed or unknown, including prayer calculation method versus mosque Iqamah schedule.

An **assistance permission** records what kind of unsolicited support, if any, the person explicitly permits; it never alters what is religiously obligatory.

## 4. The decision protocol

Apply this order for each potential recommendation:

1. **Recognize an actual decision.** Is there a stated goal or meaningful emerging conflict? If not, leave the plan as it is.
2. **Collect minimum relevant facts.** What is definitely scheduled, what changed, who is affected, how much time is available, and which evidence is trusted?
3. **Check applicable religious boundaries.** Only rely on reviewed rules and the individual's relevant recognized practice; otherwise present neutral information or refer. Never silently choose a contested method.
4. **Protect practical boundaries.** Account for safety, rest, dependents, legitimate commitments, mobility and travel. Check whether the conflict is real rather than apparent.
5. **Generate low-disruption options.** First attempt to preserve both legitimate commitments. Then consider moving explicitly flexible activities or voluntary intentions. Retain an option to make no change.
6. **Choose a permissible response type.** Inform; suggest; ask one materially useful question; request approval to reorganize; preserve; abstain; refer; or remain silent.
7. **Obtain permission before meaningful changes.** Never reschedule another person's commitment, add an obligation, expand monitoring or modify selected religious guidance unilaterally.
8. **Use corrections, not moral judgments, for adaptation.** Observed mismatches invite confirmation. Repeated dismissals invite less intervention, but numerical thresholds need testing.

A **religiously consequential decision** requires scholarly eligibility and applicability checks, not just a scheduling optimization. A **voluntary plan adjustment** can be offered under a more permissive process but must remain user-controlled.

### Three cases that must not be collapsed

- **The person intended something but a practical conflict prevented it:** explore a verified, feasible alternative if available.
- **The person does not want optional assistance now:** preserve that preference without guilt.
- **The religion imposes a binding duty but applicability is disputed or affected by circumstances:** present reviewed information and refer; do not pretend that personal motivation or calendar fit determines the religious ruling.

## 5. Initial understanding — proposed eight question areas

These are question **areas**, not a mandated eight-screen or four-minute assessment; test cognitive burden and every question's practical value.

| Area | Initial question | Decision changed | When to ask more |
|---|---|---|---|
| Desired improvement | What feels difficult or out of balance right now? | Determines focus; avoids all-domain optimization | When an actual new goal is chosen |
| Roles and responsibilities | What currently takes up your week (multiple roles allowed)? | Selects relevant context and cases | When roles change |
| Schedule pattern | Fixed, day-specific, shift-based, self-employed, caregiving, unpredictable? | Determines recurring vs short-horizon planning | When a new roster or term arrives |
| Existing commitments | Which blocks are firm, flexible or uncertain? | Protects other people's rights and planned commitments | When a conflict cannot be resolved |
| Sleep and recovery | What are the broad recovery windows, or are they unpredictable? | Limits early/late voluntary suggestions | After a schedule or capacity change |
| Prayer planning | What prayer timing approach and kind of assistance do you use or want? | Separates clock windows, mosque preferences and reminders | Ask jurisprudential method only when it matters; never guess |
| Care/protected time | What time or responsibilities should not be interrupted or filled? | Protects dependents, private time and sleep | Ask functional details only when relevant |
| Assistance permissions | On-demand, gentle, proactive, and which types of prompts? | Controls suggestions and silence | When user requests or corrects it |

**Stage after these areas:** reflect back a draft understanding under three labels: *you told me*, *needs confirmation*, and *unknown*. Ask the person to correct major errors and approve consequential planning assumptions. Unknown data yields narrower recommendations, not invented certainty.

**Progressive questions:** preferred mosque and confirmation of Iqamah only when congregation assistance is chosen; accessibility requirements in functional terms rather than diagnoses; location only when geographic timing or travel is necessary; Qur'an routine only if desired; optional phone-use assistance only with permission; Ramadan/travel questions only when relevant. No mandatory information about religious observance history, marital status, menstruation, family medical records or private phone contents.

## 6. Assistance modes

- **On demand:** only requested information and expressly selected essential reminders; no spontaneous coaching.
- **Gentle:** occasional assistance tied to chosen commitments, within user-defined limits.
- **Proactive:** may present proposed daily arrangements and respond to changes, but every consequential modification remains explainable, dismissible and subject to approval.

Do not translate these modes into different religious duties. Caps such as *three prompts per day* or *two dismissals in fourteen days* from one supplied report are candidate experiments, not agreed scientific constants.

## 7. Concrete example cases and expected behavior

**Case A — Fajr / variable sleep.** A night-shift worker requests help attending Fajr and also needs daytime recovery. The system keeps the applicable prayer timing requirement distinct from optional post-Fajr routines. It may help arrange a realistic prayer opportunity without treating the night shift as blanket permission to postpone or combine prayers. It does not insist on staying awake until Ishraq.

**Case B — Dhuhr / a meeting.** A user's work meeting crosses the Iqamah at Mosque A, but the prayer window remains open. The system clarifies the distinction, checks if Mosque B has a current confirmed congregation time, and gives a practical option if feasible. If congregation times are stale, it does not invent one. The meeting itself does not automatically excuse the religious duty.

**Case C — Family care / Qur'an.** A parent is caring for a sick child during their voluntary recitation period. The session is optional and can lapse or move if the parent requests; no streak penalty or backlog accumulates.

**Case D — Travel / differing fiqh.** Travel plans may affect prayer rules. The system can present reviewed, named interpretations relevant to the situation, ask which recognized guidance the person follows where appropriate, and refer complex applicability questions. It never selects combining prayer as a convenience optimization.

**Case E — Menstruation and privacy.** A person who voluntarily shares a relevant circumstance should not be counted as missing prayers that are not required in that circumstance, consistent with Sahih Muslim 335c. The system never assumes or infers menstrual status from patterns and never makes private disclosure mandatory. Source: https://sunnah.com/muslim:335c

**Case F — Already balanced routine.** Someone reports that obligations, rest, family and work are already manageable. The system does not invent improvement goals. It can remain on-demand and silent.

**Case G — Phone distraction.** If a person chooses to reduce unwanted scrolling, offer optional environmental friction or precommitted phone-free periods without covertly collecting device history. If removing a distraction would block emergency contact or essential work, do not enforce it.

**Case H — Mixed hard commitments.** A hospital worker faces an urgent patient event during a previously intended prayer opportunity. The system prioritizes immediate safety and avoids interruptions, recognizes the need for accurate scholarly guidance afterward, and does not declare a dispensation based merely on the job title.

## 8. Reconciled differences across the research reports

| Topic | Variations across reports | Adopted ruling for the **product design** |
|---|---|---|
| Onboarding length | 8, 10–11, 15 questions | Eight core question *areas* with conditional follow-ups; test retention and plan usefulness |
| Intervention default | Silence-first vs active help | User-chosen assistance intensity; silence available in every mode |
| Reminder limits | Three per day and dismiss-after-two suggested | Keep adjustable; do not treat thresholds as facts without testing |
| Prayer-time uncertainty | 'Cautious window' vs ask/abstain | Ask for reviewed relevant method; no unreviewed cross-school intersection fallback |
| Religious rulebook | Person owns rules vs scholarly gate | Document selected recognized scholarly interpretation; user cannot invent binding rulings by preference |
| Mosque timetable | User-confirmed only vs generic verification | Evidence-tier model: authority, recency, type (adhan vs iqamah), confirmation; abstain when uncertain |
| Changing plans | Autonomous adaptations vs approval | Propose consequential modifications; opt-in harmless changes can be reversible |
| Prayer attendance / missed prayers | Universal defaults vs jurisdiction-sensitive | No guilt judgments, no fabricated concessions, no universal mosque-attendance ruling |
| Tracking Qur'an/prayer | Avoid any metrics vs practical continuity | Allow voluntary bookmarks/practical progress; reject worship rankings, spiritual scores and coercive streaks |
| Universal support | Cross-lifestyle universal vs Sunni-heavy research | Universal model, religious feature coverage only after relevant scholarly verification |

**Critical correction:** a schedule model must not place all faith duties, family duties, rest and work into one fixed linear priority list. The legal and ethical status of any conflict depends on particulars and applicable legal interpretations. A hierarchy is useful for *finding questions and feasible options*, not for assigning an absolute worth to lives or people.

## 9. Independent validation gates

### Gate A — Religious correctness (mandatory before religious advice)
A qualified multi-tradition scholarly process reviews exact wording, primary references, hadith grading, named schools, conditions, concessions and abstention cases. Include women's scholarship and prayer-time/astronomical expertise as relevant. Disputed items should not be silently converted into global defaults. Publish traceable corrections to reviewed content.

### Gate B — Personal understanding and usability
Test whether users can answer, skip or correct each onboarding area, accurately identify what the system knows, and understand which outputs are plans rather than religious rulings. Use cognitive interviews and scenarios with students, parents, shift workers, business owners, women and people with accessibility needs.

### Gate C — Real-world feasibility
Construct an independently reviewed case bank with time windows, commitments, uncertain mosque data, travel, caregiving, changing schedules, fatigue and genuine no-action cases. A system passes only if it recognizes its limits as well as producing useful options.

### Gate D — Effectiveness and harm
Compare against accurate prayer information plus an ordinary calendar/manual plan, not against no assistance alone. Candidate outcomes: user-defined preventable conflict burden, perceived balance and autonomy, manageable sleep, unwanted device interaction and unaddressed practical obligations. Track adverse outcomes: guilt, overchecking, stress, sleep loss, family/work harm, privacy concerns and incorrect religious information. Move to randomized efficacy claims only after feasibility and outcome measures are validated.

**Any serious religiously consequential misinformation, privacy exposure or unsafe intervention requires stopping that mechanism and independent review.**

## 10. Accepted now vs open by necessity

### Adopt as conceptual foundations now
Rights and responsibilities, non-equal balance, contextual religious applicability, feasible-option planning, meaningful onboarding, opt-in help, privacy and silence, no devotional scoring, transparent uncertainty, consented adaptation, expert-referral boundaries.

### Require independent sign-off before authoritative content or public claims
Exact rulings for congregation, Jumu'ah applicability, combining/shortening, prayer-time methods and high latitudes, menstruation/postpartum complexities, illness and hardship, and detailed inter-tradition coverage.

### Require empirical tests; not religious or historical issues
How many questions improve fit, whether planned routines survive ordinary life, which prompt frequency helps, how consented personalization compares with static planning, and whether added advice creates pressure or harmful dependence.

### Defer without weakening the daily-life foundation
Advanced character coaching, finance compliance, family mediation, zakat/Hajj guidance, career mentoring, sophisticated passive inference and social/sharing features.

## 11. Non-negotiable acceptance scenarios

A reviewer must be able to answer YES to these before treating the model as ready for behavioral specification:

1. Does it accommodate a night-shift worker without prescribing a universal morning routine?
2. Does it distinguish prayer window, congregation and optional worship?
3. Can a caregiver continue a day without shame or an accumulating voluntary backlog?
4. Can a person choose purely on-demand assistance and receive no unexpected coaching?
5. Does it refuse to silently select a contested fiqh position or invent a concession?
6. Does it avoid treating private menstrual information or inferred behavior as a mandatory input?
7. Can it explain exactly why an onboarding question changes the plan?
8. Can the first draft function reasonably when some information is unknown?
9. Does it preserve unallocated rest and family time as legitimate?
10. When the mosque timetable is stale, does it avoid claiming a verified congregation?
11. When a person changes jobs or shifts, does it seek confirmation before structural rescheduling?
12. Can an independent reviewer distinguish religious evidence from modern planning suggestions?

## 12. Final verdict

**Conceptual foundation: coherent enough to adopt as v1.0.** It fixes the system's values, boundaries, inputs, decision sequence, response types and safety posture. **Religious operational validity: pending independent scholarly review. Behavioral efficacy: untested.** These are deliberately separate statements. Further broad literature searches are unlikely to replace actual scholarly adjudication and real-life testing of the remaining open questions.
